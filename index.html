About

I study how social and educational robots can help children learn foreign languages, and I bring more than a decade of French and English teaching into that work.

My doctoral research at the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki looks at how the educational robot Ducky Code supports vocabulary learning in primary school, across English and French classes. I combine classroom observation, pre/post testing, focus groups and teacher interviews in a mixed-methods design. My background is in ICT in Education, and I am now extending this work towards AI- and LLM-enhanced language learning. I am looking for a postdoctoral position in Europe to continue this line of research.

Doctoral research · 2021 – present

Robots in the foreign-language classroom

Robot-Assisted Foreign Language Learning: The Contribution of the Ducky Code Educational Robot to Vocabulary Acquisition in Primary School

Aristotle University of Thessaloniki School of French Language & Literature Expected completion 2026

Research questions

  • RQ1Does Ducky Code help students understand new vocabulary?
  • RQ2Does it improve their ability to use new vocabulary in communicative activities?
  • RQ3Does it motivate more active participation in class?

Study design

Observed robot-assisted sessions per group · 36 in total

Methods

  • Structured classroom observation with a rated indicator framework
  • Pre/post vocabulary tests with experimental and control groups
  • Focus groups with students in the robot-assisted groups
  • Semi-structured teacher interview
  • Non-parametric statistics and thematic coding

In the classroom

Ducky Code in action

The robot’s scripts and the images it shows were written and created with ChatGPT. Each script is set in the robot’s own world, so when the target vocabulary is daily routines, the robot talks about its own day. Students were tested on the vocabulary before and after each unit with short Wordwall quizzes.

Course design sample

Boosting Speaking Fluency with AI Conversational Agents

An online course I designed and built in Articulate 360 for foreign language teachers, tutors and other language professionals. It shows how AI conversational agents can be used for speaking practice, from choosing a tool to designing activities, assessing progress and preparing learners for oral exams.

Open the course on Articulate →

Course structure · 8 lessons

  1. 1Benefits of AI conversational agents. Agent types, how they work in education, and how AI practice compares with traditional conversation practice.
  2. 2Integrating AI tools for oral practice. Choosing a tool, a five-step process for setting up a session, and strategies for keeping learners motivated.
  3. 3Designing AI-enhanced conversational activities. Aligning activities with objectives, scaffolding for different levels, lesson frameworks, and role-play, problem-solving, information-gap and debate formats.
  4. 4Assessing and adapting AI-based speaking activities. Assessment criteria, monitoring tools, feedback from AI, self-reflection, peers and the teacher, and a continuous-improvement cycle.
  5. 5Knowledge check. A four-question quiz on lessons 1 to 4.
  6. 6Preparing learners for oral exams. Simulating exam tasks for IELTS, ECCE, ECPE and FCE, practising under time limits, and using AI feedback with professional judgement.
  7. 7Summary. Seven key strategies for educators.
  8. 8Final quiz. A five-question scenario-based quiz on exam preparation.

Learning design choices

  • Clear learning objectives at the start of each lesson, with a numbered overview
  • A mix of interactive elements: flashcards, tabs, accordions, step-by-step guides, drag-and-drop matching and short-answer items
  • Audio samples of an AI agent and of AI-generated speaking feedback
  • Practical examples, scaffolds and lesson frameworks that teachers can reuse
  • Scenario-based quiz questions that test judgement rather than recall
  • Emphasis on the teacher’s role, privacy and the limits of AI feedback

Research directions

Where my work is heading

LLM-enhanced language learning

Bringing large language models into language teaching, building on classroom evaluation methods from my robot studies.

Spoken interaction and pronunciation feedback

Multimodal feedback that combines LLMs with speech and prosody analysis, drawing on the spoken interaction in robot-assisted lessons.

Human–robot interaction for young learners

How children engage with social and educational robots, and what makes a robot useful to the teacher as well as the student.

Evaluating learning technology in real classrooms

Rubric-based observation, pilot studies and teacher feedback as tools for judging whether a technology helps learners.
